## What one maintenance request costs you today

Every property manager knows this loop. A tenant reports a problem by text, call, portal or email. Someone reads it, figures out what is wrong and does some basic troubleshooting. Then the back and forth starts: photos from the tenant, calls to vendors for availability and an estimate, an owner approval if the job is over your limit, a notice of entry, updates in the property management software, a follow-up to confirm the work is done, owner charges from the invoice, and finally the vendor payment.

For us, that came out to about **two to three hours per request**. Multiply that by 800 units and you understand why property management teams keep hiring admin staff.

## How the same request runs with AI agents

We use Claude as the brain behind four agents. Each agent owns one part of the job, because one agent juggling everything makes mistakes.

### Agent 1: Intake

When a tenant creates a ticket, the intake agent reads it, pulls out the tenant, unit, issue and urgency, and goes back and forth with the tenant to clarify. Then it recommends the best vendor from our vetted list based on the trade, availability and who we used last.

Nothing reaches me until this point. The agent brings me its assessment with three options: **Yes, Edit or Cancel.**

- **Yes** means it goes ahead.
- **Edit** means the vendor choice was wrong. I give the reason and it goes into the agent's memory, so it picks better next time.
- **Cancel** means I know something the agent does not, like a different arrangement with the owner for that property.

### Agent 2: Dispatch

Once I approve, the dispatch agent contacts the vendor (usually by text, because vendors answer faster), confirms the time, puts it on my calendar, updates the task in the property management software and sends the tenant a notice of entry. I do not touch any of it. I just see the event appear on my calendar.

### Agent 3: Completion

When the vendor reports the job is done, this agent confirms with the tenant. If the tenant says it is not fixed, or the stories do not match, it escalates to me. Otherwise it logs the task as pending invoice and watches for the vendor's bill.

### Agent 4: Invoice and close-out

When the invoice arrives, this agent reads it, works out the owner charges (including any markup we add) and asks me to approve the payment. After my thumbs up, it records the bill in the property management software, pays the vendor and closes the task.

## What tenants, vendors and owners notice

Almost nothing changes from their side, which is the point.

- **Tenants** still have three touchpoints: they send a request, they get a notice of entry, and they confirm the work is done.
- **Vendors** get two: a clear request with photos, a description and our assessment, then a confirmation once they send the invoice.

What changes is the property manager's job. All the coordination in the middle is handled by the agents.

## Where people still make the call

I only step in twice per request:

1. **Approving the vendor.** Sending the wrong vendor means escalations and wasted trips, so a person checks this every time.
2. **Approving the payment.** Before any money moves, a person confirms the work is done and the charges are right.

Plus anything the agents escalate, like a tenant saying the job is not finished.

> The property manager's job becomes quality control, not admin work.

That is where I think this industry is going. Property managers will spend their time checking the agents' work, building relationships and growing the vetted vendor list, because the vendor list is what the agents dispatch from.

## What has to be connected

Your property management software is a database and an accounting system, not an operating system. Buildium, AppFolio, Rent Manager and Yardi all store your records, but they do not run the work between tenants, vendors and owners. The agents do that by connecting four things:

- **Text messaging** for vendor contact and notices of entry.
- **Email** to pick up maintenance requests and vendor invoices.
- **Calendar** to see which vendor is at which property when.
- **Property management software with write access**, so the agents can update tasks and record bills without anyone typing them in.

Getting those connections to work reliably, with approvals and a record of every action, is where most of our build time went. It is also the part Acel Cortex sets up for property management companies.

## FAQ

### Is it safe to let AI agents contact my vendors and tenants?

Yes, if the agent cannot act on the decisions that matter without you. In our setup the agent never dispatches a vendor or releases a payment without a person approving it, and every message and action is logged in the property management software.

### What happens when the AI picks the wrong vendor?

You hit Edit instead of Yes and tell it why. That reason goes into the agent's memory, so the next similar request gets a better recommendation. Over time you approve more and correct less.

### Do I need to use Claude, or will ChatGPT work?

The structure matters more than the model. We use Claude because it handles our reasoning and writing well, but the same four-agent setup works with other major models.

### How many staff does an 800-unit company need with this setup?

We run our 800 units with three employees. Their time goes to quality control, owner and tenant relationships, and work that brings in revenue, instead of the back and forth with vendors and tenants.
