## Why late rent gets harder as you grow

Chasing five late tenants a month does not feel like a big deal. Chasing 20, 30 or 50 does. The work is tedious, but you cannot skip it: if the rent is not collected, your management fee is not collected either.

The traditional process looks like this:

1. On the 2nd, you notice rent has not been paid and send a friendly reminder.
2. No response, so you send a notice through your property management software.
3. Then emails, then texts, then calls. Three to five more attempts.
4. Still nothing, so someone drafts the formal notice and mails it or posts it on the door.
5. In Ontario, you then wait 14 days before you can apply to the Landlord and Tenant Board.

At 500 or 1,000 units, the number of late tenants rises with you, and so does the work.

## How AI agents handle it now

Once it is set up, the system runs on the 2nd of every month without anyone starting it.

- **Day 1:** The system sends the notice from our property management software, then a text, an email and a call in a friendly tone.
- **Day 2:** No reply? A follow-up text and call with a firmer tone and a late reminder.
- **Day 3:** A more urgent text. The same day, the AI pulls the outstanding balance from the property management software, fills in the formal notice, emails it to the tenant and sends a copy to our team.

My team does not think about rent collection at all until day three. That copy is their signal to act.

The voice messages follow a script that escalates, roughly like this:

1. **First call:** a friendly heads-up that rent has not come in, asking them to take care of it today.
2. **Second call:** a second attempt, asking what is going on and offering to work something out.
3. **Third call:** a clear last message that if there is no payment or communication, the formal notice comes next.

Because the AI learns from your scripts and recordings, it gets closer to how you would actually speak to tenants at each stage.

## Where people still make the call

The formal notice is where a person takes over. Our property managers decide whether to knock on the door and serve it in person, drop by to see what is happening, or mail it. That depends on the property and their relationship with the tenant, so it stays a human judgment call.

Everything before that, the detecting, reminding, calling and paperwork, runs on its own.

## What has to be connected

- **A trigger** that runs on the 2nd of every month.
- **Your property management software**, through its API, so the AI can read overdue balances, tenant names and units. Buildium, AppFolio, Rent Manager and others work.
- **An AI model** to choose the tone and write each message. Any major model works, whether that is Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini.
- **Texting and voice tools** for the messages and calls. If you already send notices of entry by text, you have most of this in place.

Getting the voice calls to sound right and the notice to fill in correctly every month is where most of the setup time goes. It is also part of what Acel Cortex sets up for property management companies.

## FAQ

### Is it legal to use AI for rent reminders?

Reminders by text, email and phone are normal practice, and the AI is sending the same messages your staff would. The formal notice and how it is served must follow your province or state's rules, which is why a person on our team handles that step.

### Will tenants know they are talking to AI?

The messages and voice are trained on your own scripts and tone, so they sound like your office. What tenants notice most is that they always hear back quickly, at the same steps, every month.

### Does this work if I only have a few rentals?

Yes, but the payoff grows with size. With a handful of units, a calendar reminder may be enough. Once you are chasing 20 or more late payments a month, automating the first three days saves hours every month.
