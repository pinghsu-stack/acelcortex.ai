Can AI Replace a Property Management Team? What Actually Runs at 800 Units

Ping Hsu, Founder of Acel Cortex. Runs an 800-unit property management company.
Published Nov 13, 2026
Short answer

AI can take over most of the busywork in a property management company, but not the judgment. At our 800-unit company, AI sorts every incoming request, routes it to the right workflow and drafts the work. A person still approves anything that touches money or needs a judgment call. My own week went from over 60 hours to about 15.

What changed when AI took over the busywork

We manage 800 units. Before AI, the phone rang all day. Tenant complaints, owner complaints and vendor coordination all landed on the same few people, and on a busy day the inbox would pile up past 100 or 200 emails before anyone could get to the real work.

I was working over 60 hours a week, most of it inside the business instead of on it.

Today AI agents do most of the heavy lifting, and I work about 15 hours a week. We did not get there by buying one big tool. We got there by putting every request through the same three steps, so the right work reaches the right place and a person only steps in when it matters.

The three layers that make it work

Layer 1: Intake, sort everything before anyone reads it

Every request goes into one inbox first: emails from tenants and owners, notifications from the property management software, messages from the phone system. Then each message gets one of three labels:

  • Needs attention: someone on the team has to step in and do something.
  • FYI: good to know, no action needed. Nobody touches these until the Needs attention pile is done.
  • Noise: ads, promotions and other companies' email campaigns. These are archived automatically so they never hit the inbox.

The rule for this first layer is simple. It reads, labels and logs. It does not act. Its only job is to turn 200 messages into a short priority list.

Layer 2: Intelligence, decide what kind of work it is

The Needs attention items go to an AI model. We use Claude, but ChatGPT or any model you are comfortable with works for this step. The model reads each request, decides which part of the business it belongs to, drafts a reply or a charge, and flags where a person is needed.

One rule matters more than the rest: each request goes to exactly one area. Without that rule, the AI will sometimes give one email two labels and two agents will both try to handle it. And when the AI is not sure, it says so instead of guessing, so a person can show it the right answer for next time.

Layer 3: Execution, agents do the work and you approve

Each area has its own agent. Straightforward tasks run on their own. Anything that needs a decision comes to me first, and I approve it from my phone in Telegram before the agent acts.

Three layers, one system, and you are still in control. AI is not in control. AI is just doing the heavy lifting.

The four lanes every property management company runs on

Most property management work falls into four areas. Each one gets its own agent.

  • Leasing: prospects, applications, showings, lease offers, renewals, move-outs and notices to vacate. Leasing comes first, because without rented units there is no management fee.
  • Maintenance: repair requests, work orders, vendor scheduling and seasonal inspections that owners ask for.
  • Admin: general questions, documents and key access. Nothing about money and nothing about repairs.
  • Accounting: vendor invoices, bills, rent and payment questions, owner statements and utilities.

Accounting stays completely separate from the other agents. You are handling owners' money and charging tenants for services, and all of it ends up in your property management software, so it needs its own lane and its own checks.

Where people still make the call

AI handles the volume. People handle the judgment. On our team, a person still decides:

  • Anything that moves money or posts to an owner's ledger.
  • Anything the AI flags as low confidence.
  • Anything that needs a relationship call with an owner, tenant or vendor.

That split is the point. When AI is not confident and a person steps in early, you avoid the back and forth that eats your time and your margin.

How to start this week

You do not need to automate everything at once. Start with the sorting.

  1. Route every request into one inbox. Email, property management software notifications and phone messages all land in the same place.
  2. Add the three labels and let it run read-only. Needs attention, FYI and Noise. Watch it for a week and correct what it gets wrong.
  3. Add the four lanes. One lane per request, and have the AI say when it is unsure.
  4. Automate one lane at a time. Start with whichever lane eats the most hours, and keep approvals on until you trust it.

The sorting is the easy part. Getting agents to act reliably inside your property management software, with approvals and a record of every action, is where most of our build time went. That part is what Acel Cortex runs for property management companies.

FAQ

Can AI replace property management staff?

AI can replace most of the busywork, such as sorting the inbox, routing requests and drafting replies, but not the judgment. At our 800-unit company, people still approve anything involving money or a judgment call. The result was fewer hours, not a company with no people: my own week went from over 60 hours to about 15.

Is it safe to let AI handle owner money?

Only with approvals. Keep accounting in its own lane, separate from the other agents, and have a person approve anything that posts to an owner's ledger or the property management software.

Which AI model should I use?

Any major model can handle the sorting and drafting. We use Claude. ChatGPT works too. The structure matters more than the model: one inbox, three labels, four lanes and human approval.

Does this work with my property management software?

Yes. The intake layer pulls from your inbox, so it works with any property management software that sends email notifications. Acel Cortex runs on top of Buildium, AppFolio, Rentvine, Rent Manager and other major platforms with no migration.

How long does it take to set up?

You can set up the sorting yourself in a week. Acel Cortex setup for the full system takes about two weeks.
