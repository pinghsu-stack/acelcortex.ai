## Why property management companies run out of cash

When I started, selling management contracts was hard and cash flow was worse. Every time I hired, cash dipped hard before it recovered. Keeping the company financially healthy was the toughest part of the business.

The problem was the order I sold things in. Most property managers chase the monthly management fee, because they want recurring income. But a management fee is small, often $100 to $300 per unit per month, and it takes a long time to add up.

Alex Hormozi's *$100M Money Models* describes four pieces of a business: an **attraction offer**, an **upsell**, a **downsell** and **continuity** (recurring income). I found by accident that property management fits it well, as long as you put the pieces in the right order.

## 1. Attraction offer: lead with leasing

Leasing fees run from half a month's rent to a full month's rent depending on your market. In mine, that is usually $3,000 to $4,000 per household, sometimes $4,500.

I offer owners 25 percent off the leasing fee if they give us the listing exclusively, with no other realtors or leasing agents. We market it ourselves, including on Facebook Marketplace. The owner saves money and gets a qualified tenant, so it is a win for both of us.

The math: at $3,000 rent and 75 percent of a month, that is **$2,250 per lease**. Four or five leases is around $9,000. To earn that from management fees at $300 a month, you would need 30 more units.

So early on, I stopped focusing on management fees and focused on leasing. I even turned down owners who wanted to do their own leasing and hand us only the management afterwards, because that leaves us the heavy lifting after they keep the profit.

## 2. Upsells: the work leasing uncovers

Placing a tenant naturally triggers upsells. Walk through a rental before move-in and you will almost always find painting, cosmetic fixes or landscaping. Painting is my favorite: it makes the unit feel fresh for the new tenant, and the margin is high if you have your own technicians. We also coordinate preventive maintenance, like HVAC work, at the same time.

## 3. Continuity: the management fee

A happy tenant in a well-prepared unit is less likely to default. That steady rent is where the management fee comes in, and it is sticky. Owners rarely switch property managers once things are running well.

The point is not to ignore recurring income. It is to cover your marketing and setup costs first, when the owner is most motivated to sign and willing to pay the most.

## Why the order matters so much

Picture a company adding three doors a month. If it lives on management fees alone, recurring income takes about 24 months to get high enough to support the business. Most property managers run out of cash long before month 19 or month 24, and either burn out or change models.

When you lead with leasing, cash comes in from the start. In our model, a company focused on leasing collected about **$200,000 more cash in its first 24 months** than one focused only on management fees. That is before counting maintenance, project management, evictions or helping owners buy and sell.

So when I consult other property managers, I flip their model: focus on leasing commissions first, keep overhead low as long as possible, and hire only as recurring income grows.

## 4. Raise your prices

Hormozi talks about raising prices constantly. Most of us start cheap just to win clients. But property management is sticky. Once owners trust your service, you can raise your fee a point or two, or charge 25 to 50 percent more to handle another pain point, like paying their utilities.

Do that across 20, 30 or 40 clients and revenue grows across the whole portfolio. Put that money back into marketing, which brings more leasing, and repeat.

## FAQ

### How much does a property management company make per lease?

It depends on your market. Leasing fees are usually half a month to a full month of rent. In our market that works out to roughly $2,250 to $4,500 per lease.

### How long does it take for management fees to cover a property management company's costs?

Adding about three doors a month, management fees alone can take around two years to cover the business. That is why leasing income matters so much in the first two years.

### Should I give a discount on leasing fees?

It can work if you get something in return. We give 25 percent off in exchange for an exclusive listing, so we control the marketing and the timeline.
