Most people, even experienced investors, think property management is low margin and high effort. On paper, you earn 8 to 10 percent of rent and deal with tenants, rent, repairs and drama in return. It does not look worth it.

That view is wrong. We run over 800 units with three employees, and here are four reasons property management is a better business than it looks.

## 1. The management fee is only the baseline

The 8 to 10 percent fee is what you earn for standing by. It is recurring income, not your whole revenue. Property management has many other ways to earn:

- **Before a tenant moves in:** repairs and upgrades to get the unit rent-ready.
- **Marketing:** a fee to advertise the unit.
- **Leasing:** a tenant placement fee when you find the right tenant.
- **Move-in:** small items at the start of a tenancy, charged to the tenant or owner depending on how you package it.
- **Ongoing maintenance:** inspections, smoke detectors, furnace, AC, roof, exterior work and landscaping. Owners, especially out-of-town investors, cannot do this themselves, so they pay you to.

## 2. AI keeps the extra work from eating the margin

All those services make the business complex, and complexity usually means more staff. That is the old way. We first moved work to virtual assistants, then built automation and AI agents for:

- **Maintenance:** AI handles first contact with tenants, troubleshoots based on our procedures, gathers details, removes the emotion from the message, and suggests a vendor. Once we approve, it sends the notice of entry and books the vendor on our calendar.
- **Accounting:** an agent reads each vendor invoice, updates pricing and descriptions in the property management software and uploads it. A second agent reviews it, flags problems to the property manager, and our finance manager pays vendors on payday.
- **Leasing:** our staff do the showings. AI handles the paperwork, from putting reports together and sending them to owners for approval to the lease signing.

With that in place, our margin more than doubled compared with where a typical property management company sits, without hiring more people or burning out.

## 3. Your owners are your best source of deals

Property management is one of the best lead sources in real estate. Your clients, whether mom-and-pop or seasoned investors, rarely stop at one property. When they grow, you grow. And if you also run a brokerage, they become repeat clients.

In 2017 and 2018, I counted the sales transactions my 40 clients did, and **I had missed 19 of them**. If I had caught even half, that would have been about 10 more transactions that year, worth over $150,000 in my market.

Owners can also become capital partners. Some cannot buy another property, but have $50,000 to $150,000 to lend or invest. That is how I bought a sixplex when I did not have much money: I raised capital from three clients, bought it with cash, renovated it and doubled their return. That building led to an investment firm that flips and holds properties, which in turn feeds the brokerage and the property management company.

The same applies if you work in mortgages or inspections. Your property management clients feed the rest of your business.

## 4. The business is worth more than it used to be

Property management companies have traditionally sold for one to two times earnings, valued like any other service business. Use virtual assistants, the right software and AI agents to cut labor costs, and you raise net operating income. Combined with a business that does not depend on a big team, that pushes the valuation multiple up. I expect private equity to start buying property management companies for exactly this reason.

## FAQ

### How much does a property management company make per door?

The management fee is usually 8 to 10 percent of rent, but that is only the baseline. Leasing fees, maintenance, upgrades and other services add significantly more per door over a year.

### What is the most profitable service in property management?

Leasing, because it pays a large fee upfront. Maintenance and upgrades are next, especially if you have your own technicians and can keep the margin.

### Is it worth starting a property management company?

It can be, if you treat it as more than the management fee. Build in leasing and maintenance revenue, use your owner relationships for sales and investment deals, and use automation so the admin work does not grow with every door.
