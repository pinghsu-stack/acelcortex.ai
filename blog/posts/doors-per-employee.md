## The metric that keeps property managers unprofitable

Most property managers plan staffing around one number: **one person can manage 50 to 70 doors**, or maybe 120 to 150 for condos. That may have been true ten years ago. If you still run your company on it today, you are probably not very profitable.

I have run a property management company since 2016. We now have over 800 units with three employees, and **one person can handle 250 to 300 units** without feeling overwhelmed. Here are the four changes that got us there.

## Why hiring kills margin as you grow

Between 2016 and 2018, every time we took on a new portfolio, the same thing happened: onboarding, admin, inspections, and the repairs those inspections uncover. Each wave meant another hire. More payroll, more management, and suddenly the business did not feel profitable anymore.

We even had to slow down onboarding so we would not have to add overhead. That is when we started asking how to bring new data in without burying our staff.

## 1. Data should not be handled by people

Our first fix was to hand importing and data entry to a virtual assistant. Then we realized most of it should not be done by a person at all. It should be one click with a proper workflow.

Two examples:

- **E-transfers.** In Canada, not every tenant pays through the portal. Many send an e-transfer. Those payment emails now get recorded on the tenant's ledger automatically.
- **Vendor invoices.** Invoices come in by email, AI reads the invoice number and details, files it, logs it in a sheet, and tells the owner and tenant. We just double check. At 5 to 10 minutes per invoice and 10 to 20 invoices a day, that is a lot of hours back.

The rule we set early: **everyone on the core team should be doing revenue work**, like quoting upsells, talking to owners, asking for referrals and winning new leasing. Admin work goes to automation.

Once every data entry task has a workflow, adding doors no longer means adding people. That is where the margin comes from.

## 2. Your property management software is a database, not your operating system

I used to try to run everything inside our property management software. It never worked the way I wanted. I suggested features to the vendor, but waiting for them to build what we needed for maintenance and owner reporting was never going to happen fast enough.

So we changed how we think about it. Buildium, AppFolio and Yardi are great at ledgers and trust accounting. That is their job. Scheduling and communication will never be their priority.

Now the property management software holds tenants, owners and the trust account. The company's own books live in QuickBooks. Vendor scheduling runs on Google Calendar, because we already live in Gmail. And every transfer of data between them is automated.

## 3. AI is the first point of contact, not your team

Burnout in property management mostly comes from emotional messages. A student reports a burned-out bulb, but the message says the room is dark, they have exams and something bad will happen if it is not fixed in 24 hours. Meanwhile the owner may push back on the repair. Your team absorbs all of it.

So our core team is never the first point of contact. AI receives the message, removes the emotion, summarizes the priority, category and issue, and asks the tenant a few troubleshooting questions. We just approve, edit or skip.

About **30 percent of the time**, the tenant solves it themselves after those questions, and we close the request without a vendor, our team or the owner. AI also catches mistakes, like a notice of entry that does not give the required 24 hours. Overall, it removes most of the daily aggravation for everyone.

## 4. Documented, automated workflows make the business worth more

Workflows and AI do not just make the business more profitable day to day. They make it more sellable. Property management companies typically sell for a low multiple of earnings, often one to three times. I believe a company that runs on documented processes and AI, without depending on a big team, can be valued well above that.

Property management is a naturally growing business. Owners hesitate to grow because more doors means more moving parts and more voices. Take the emotion and the busywork out of the day, and growth becomes something you can handle.

## FAQ

### How many units can one property manager handle with AI?

On our team, one person handles 250 to 300 units. That depends on AI handling first contact and on data entry being automated, so the person is approving and deciding, not typing and chasing.

### What should I automate first to stop hiring?

Data entry. Payment records, vendor invoices and moving information between systems are the easiest wins, and they free your team for revenue work.

### Will tenants get worse service if AI answers first?

In our experience, no. AI replies fast and calmly, asks the right questions, and solves about 30 percent of maintenance requests through basic troubleshooting. Anything it cannot handle still reaches a person.
