If your company is stuck somewhere between 100 and 500 doors, you have probably noticed that more doors does not mean more profit. More doors means more problems, which means more hires.

I was there. What I eventually learned is that **it was not a staffing problem. It was an accounting flow problem.** Fixing it is how we got to over 800 units with three employees.

## The four accounting problems that cap your growth

### 1. Treating your property management software as all-in-one

Buildium, AppFolio, Propertyware and the rest are accounting software at heart. They manage the trust account, trust ledgers and lease ledgers. They try to get you to do everything inside them, but they are not built for it.

So in practice you schedule vendors in Google Calendar, call tenants and owners from another tool, manage projects elsewhere and run company finances in QuickBooks. If you expect one platform to do it all, the friction will cap you around 100 to 150 doors, and you will start dreading new clients.

Accept that you will use several tools, and make them work together.

### 2. Double and triple data entry

Once your company books live in a separate system, data has to be moved between the two. That usually means hiring people for double and triple entry. Your property managers stop managing properties and relationships and start doing data entry, which is expensive.

### 3. Invoices and markups slip through

Adding a markup to some invoices is normal, especially when your team or technicians spent a long time on a job. At 5 to 10 invoices a week, it is manageable. At 50 to 100, things get missed.

Miss one or two invoices and the company pays out of pocket. Those two invoices can easily wipe out the markup from your next 10 to 15.

### 4. No weekly profit view

Do you know if you made money last week, or are you guessing? Your property management software cannot show you a real weekly profit and loss for your company. I tried many times.

## The fix in three steps

### Step 1: One inbox for invoices, read by AI

Create an email address used only for invoices, like invoices@ or ap@ your domain. Every vendor invoice goes there.

An AI agent reads every invoice that arrives, including handwritten ones (it reads handwriting better than people do), and logs it in a Google Sheet: property address, work description, amount, any duplicates for the same property, and the matching task from the property management software. That sheet becomes your dashboard. You can see at a glance what you owe vendors this week.

### Step 2: Review, add the markup, click post

Once a week, the owner reviews the sheet. You can usually tell at a glance whether a number makes sense. Enter the final amount with markup in its own column and mark it ready to post.

A second agent takes it from there: it posts the charge in the property management software, updates the task, records the payment and posts it to QuickBooks. Because you review everything in one sitting instead of one invoice at a time, you do not get distracted and miss things. It takes me **less than 30 minutes a week**.

### Step 3: A weekly profit check

A third step builds a weekly and monthly profit view: what we paid vendors, next to what we charged owners and successfully posted. As long as the number is positive every week, I leave it alone. When it goes negative, we dig in.

Eventually this weekly review can move to an operations or finance manager, so the owner is out of it entirely.

## What changes

Once the accounting flow runs itself, your property managers stop managing accounting. They manage properties and relationships. That is how you grow past 200 doors without growing payroll at the same rate.

## FAQ

### Why do property management companies stop growing at 200 doors?

Usually because every new door adds data entry, invoices and coordination that someone has to do by hand. Payroll grows with the door count, so profit does not. Automating the accounting flow breaks that link.

### Should I run company finances in my property management software?

No. Keep the trust account in your property management software and run the company's own books in a separate system like QuickBooks. Then automate the data moving between them.

### What is the biggest hidden cost when scaling a property management company?

Missed owner charge-backs. Each missed invoice is money the company pays out of pocket, and at 50 to 100 invoices a week a few misses can wipe out the markup on many others.
