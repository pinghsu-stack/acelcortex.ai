Most owners think growing means hiring. In the AI era, it is the opposite. We run over 800 units on a lean team, and these are the four things I would tell any property manager starting with AI.

## 1. Write the SOP before you automate anything

Before prompts and tools, ask: do you have a written standard operating procedure for this task? If you would need a manual to hand it to a new employee, you need it for AI too.

When we are burned out, we want to automate whatever we hate most. That is the wrong place to start. Start with tasks where the process already exists, which is usually admin and accounting work. Hand the process to AI, then let AI help you improve it.

## 2. Use AI to do the work, not just answer questions

Most people still use AI as a chat: ask a question, get an answer, done. The shift is trusting AI to carry out a whole process for you.

It will make mistakes at first, especially if your process has gaps. Part of the job is learning how to train it and communicate with it, the same way you would with a new hire, until it can run the task on its own.

## 3. Separate the thinking from the doing

Different AI tools are good at different things. Using only one is like having only one kind of talent on your team.

- **Thinking:** I use Claude as my "brain." I keep separate projects for different parts of the business so the context stays clean, and I use it to plan and refine processes.
- **Doing:** I use an AI agent that can work in my email and browser to carry out the plan. It drafts emails, gathers information from other systems and downloads attachments, even from software with no API.

It is the same as managing people. You do the thinking, write clear instructions, hand them off and check progress. When the agent gets stuck, you go back to planning and improve the process.

## A real example: tenant application reviews

We get a lot of rental applications through our leasing agents. Reviewing one meant collecting the application, credit report, employment letter and bank statements, chasing what was missing and making sure it all matched. **It often took over a week to approve someone**, and we lost a few strong applicants because of it.

So I asked Claude how to compress that to a day. I reviewed its plan, had it turn the plan into instructions, and handed those to the agent. Now, for every new application:

- The agent pulls the documents from our property management software.
- It assesses everything and emails us a summary.
- If anything is missing, it contacts the applicant to request it.

Nobody logs in or opens files anymore. We read the summary and make the call.

## 4. Automate tasks, not departments

Do not try to automate the whole leasing or maintenance department at once. Every department has decisions that still need you.

Pick one task, ideally one where a mistake will not hurt your reputation, so you can learn safely. Plan it carefully, check the agent's work closely at first, and give it more control as it proves itself, until it only comes to you when there is a problem.

Do that and in 30 to 60 days you will not be buried in admin work. You will have time for marketing and sales.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between an AI chatbot and an AI agent?

A chatbot answers questions. An agent takes actions: it reads your email, gathers documents, updates systems and reports back. For property management, the agent is what saves real time.

### Which task should a property manager automate first with AI?

One you already have a written process for and where a mistake is easy to catch, like reviewing rental applications or sorting the inbox. Avoid starting with anything that moves owner money.

### Do I need technical skills to use AI agents?

You need clear processes more than technical skills. If you can write instructions a new hire could follow, you can train an agent. Connecting agents to your property management software and phone systems is the more technical part, and that is what Acel Cortex sets up.
