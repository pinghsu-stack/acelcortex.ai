## The expensive mistake: missed owner charges

Every property manager processes vendor invoices and bills the cost back to owners. And almost every property manager, at some point, misses a charge. When that happens, the company pays out of pocket, and it comes straight out of your profit.

The process looks simple on paper. In practice it is not, and that is why charges slip through.

## How the software expects you to do it

Property management software like Buildium and AppFolio has a standard flow:

1. A work order is created and the vendor completes the job.
2. The vendor sends an invoice.
3. You record the bill.
4. You pay the vendor by EFT or check.
5. The bill shows up on the owner's statement.

## How most property managers actually do it

That standard flow does not fit how most companies operate. Often you do not want the owner to see which vendor you used, maybe because you are testing a new one or you negotiated a price and keep the difference. The standard flow also makes it hard to add a markup.

So most property managers, I would say over 90 percent, use a workaround: they charge the owner's account as a bill paid to the management company, then pay the vendor separately. That keeps a buffer between you and the owner, but it turns a few steps into seven:

1. Receive the invoice by text or email.
2. Rewrite the vendor's description so it fits the owner statement.
3. Create the bill.
4. Pay it to the management company.
5. Pay the vendor separately, by EFT or check.
6. Record that payment in your company books, like QuickBooks, which is not even in the property management software.
7. Check that everything matches.

That is why growing companies end up hiring a VA, bookkeeper or accountant just to process invoices, which adds another layer someone has to check. And charges still get missed.

## How AI handles it now

I wanted AI to take all the back and forth: rewriting descriptions in our format, downloading and uploading bills. The one thing I would not hand over without a check is matching the invoice to the work order, because without an approval step, money just flows out.

Now it works like this:

1. **Forward the invoice** to a dedicated email address. That starts the process.
2. **Verify.** The AI matches the invoice to the work order and asks me about anything unclear. I confirm the match, the owner charge with any markup, and the vendor amount.
3. **Pay the vendor and double check.** The AI has already created the bill and posted the owner charge, so the extra steps of paying the management company and uploading invoices twice are gone. When I pay the vendor, I check that it posted correctly on the owner statement.

Seven steps down to three.

## Catch missed charges every week

A second AI agent audits the flow and gives us weekly numbers. The math is simple: owner charges posted minus what we paid vendors. If a vendor payment is bigger than the owner charge, something was missed, and we fix it that week instead of discovering it months later.

## FAQ

### Can AI replace a bookkeeper in a property management company?

Not entirely. AI handles the repetitive invoice and charge-back work, but you still want a bookkeeper reconciling the trust and operating accounts. We use AI for the processing and a bookkeeper for the monthly reconciliation.

### Is it safe to let AI process vendor invoices?

Only with an approval step. AI does the matching and data entry, but a person confirms the invoice matches the work order and approves the amounts before any money moves.

### Should owners see which vendor did the work?

That is your call. Many property managers bill the owner through the management company so they can keep a markup and avoid back and forth about vendor choices. Whichever way you do it, keep it consistent and documented in your management agreement.
