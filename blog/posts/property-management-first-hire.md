If you are at 40 or 50 doors and feeling overwhelmed, that is normal. Things start breaking at that size. What matters is that your next hire moves the business forward, not backwards. AI has changed the right hiring order for property managers, so here is the order I would follow if I started over.

## The mistake that cost me two years

When I was at about 20 doors, I wanted out of the day to day. Dealing with tenants and owners is mentally draining, so my first hire was an admin to talk to them for me. My second was a leasing manager, because coordinating showings and dealing with no-shows drove me crazy.

Both decisions were emotional. I was getting rid of tasks I did not want to do. That left me doing the bookkeeping, which I am not good at, while overhead went up and revenue did not. The company started bleeding cash and I was anxious all the time without realizing I was solving the wrong problem.

That hiring order set the business back about two years.

The lesson: early on, the business is fighting to survive, and cash flow is everything. Every hire needs to pay for itself quickly. And as the owner, you have the highest close rate in the company, because you care the most. So you should be the one doing leasing and client calls.

## The order I would hire in now

### 1. A remote bookkeeper (plus AI for the inbox)

My first hire would be a bookkeeper, most likely a virtual assistant who has worked in other property management companies. They keep the trust, operating and tax accounts organized and reconciled monthly, or even weekly. That keeps you accountable for growth and catches expenses you did not know about.

At the same time, let AI take the admin role I made the mistake of hiring for. Spend a day or two cleaning up your inbox, send every request into it, and have AI sort what is urgent, what is just FYI and what is noise. I still use this setup at 800 units.

With a bookkeeper watching the books and AI filtering the inbox, your job is simple: bring in revenue. More leasing, more owner calls, more properties.

### 2. A maintenance technician (around 75 to 100 doors)

Your first full-time hire should be a handyman or technician. It solves two problems:

- **Margin.** Paying outside vendors means that money goes straight in and out. With your own technician, you keep a good share of it.
- **Control.** Outside vendors have other clients and cannot always prioritize you. Your own technician works on your schedule, so you can handle urgent issues first and cut down on tenant complaints.

Then go hard on leasing for the next 6 to 12 months. It is the highest-income work in a property management company, and it creates upsells. When you list a unit and see repairs, exterior work, lawn care or landscaping, you can pitch it to the owner and your technician keeps the margin.

### 3. A leasing agent (when you fall behind on rentals)

When you can no longer keep up with renting units, hire a leasing agent. Do not stop leasing yourself. You still need to check quality, and you keep more of the commission on the leases you close.

The technician and the leasing agent are both what I call billable roles. Most of their work can be billed to owners, so as long as you manage them well, they earn more than they cost.

### 4. A maintenance coordinator (your first manager)

By now, more units bring more leasing and more work for your technician, and owners start asking you to help them buy and sell. This is when you hire a maintenance coordinator, your first management role, to free up your time for the highest-value work.

## The rule behind the order

You are not filling gaps for work you dislike. You are filling gaps so you can keep doing higher-value work. Look at your current team and ask which roles are generating revenue or protecting cash, and which ones only made your day more comfortable.

## FAQ

### When should a property management company make its first full-time hire?

Around 75 to 100 doors, and the first full-time hire should be a maintenance technician. Before that, a part-time remote bookkeeper and AI for the inbox cover most of what you need.

### Should my first hire be an admin assistant?

Not anymore. That was my mistake. An admin adds overhead without adding revenue, and AI can now sort the inbox and draft replies, which is most of what an early admin does.

### Should the bookkeeper be local?

No. A remote bookkeeper with experience at other property management companies understands trust accounting and costs less. What matters is that they reconcile at least monthly.
